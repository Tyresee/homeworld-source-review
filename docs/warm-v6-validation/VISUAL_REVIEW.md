# Warm-v6 and warm floors visual acceptance

**PASS.** All 19 final captures were individually inspected against the supplied 1440 x 900 upstream references: five overall views, six wood finishes and eight tile finishes. No blocking visual repair is requested.

The five overall views pass for background, walls, lighting, exterior and furniture. Their new catalog-default floors intentionally differ from the old warm-v6 floor roles, so those five floor colors are not compared directly. Each of the 14 individual floor products passes against its corresponding product reference.

The light oak, dark walnut, pale washed oak and redder narrow cherry retain distinct color and brightness levels. Both herringbone finishes retain the connected zigzag pattern. Wide and narrow boards have visibly different density. All eight tile colors retain the intended warm or neutral color family and a large, subdued square grid. Bed fabric, rugs, kitchen surfaces, wood furniture and the robot retain their own texture and color identities.

The following differences are accepted:

- Wood board variation and texture phase differ from the temporary upstream prototype. The sampled floor-region mean differences are about 1.28-2.71 Delta E76; apparent width, pattern and overall color remain comparable.
- True 1.4 m square tiles cover the 5.6 by 5.2 m floor as 4 by about 3.714 cells. The old reference stretched its grid to 4 by 4. The changed depth pitch and final partial cell are a reasonable physical-scale correction.
- Some individual tile references have slightly different wall appearance from the standard warm-v6 configuration. The largest sampled wall difference is about **3.12 Delta E76** in the blue-tile view; the current wall is slightly warmer. The standard five-view wall context otherwise matches closely, so standard warm-v6 wall and lighting settings remain authoritative. No per-tile wall or lighting retuning is requested.
- The new bathroom floor uses a clean full surface without the old perimeter accent squares. The separate raw warm-v6 reproduction retains the old accents. Bright exterior grass is a known upstream limitation and remains visible.

The five raw warm-v6 reproduction views were also separately inspected and pass. For the final five overall views, sampled background-region means match and the largest sampled wall or furniture difference is about 0.077 Delta E76. The eight tile floor samples match their corresponding references.

These are manual visual conclusions supported by exported sRGB pixel diagnostics. They are not pixel-perfect requirements or measurements of GPU cost, frame rate, loading performance, physical-display calibration, account persistence or classroom use. The machine-readable result is in `public-validation.json`.
